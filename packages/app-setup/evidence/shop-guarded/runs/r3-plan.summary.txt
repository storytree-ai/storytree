## What's planned
There are four new stories: **Accounts**, **Orders**, **Admin** and **Reviews**. Search and stock are added to the existing **Browsing** story. In total that's 16 new parts, each with tests that check your exact wording and `data-test` names. Each piece of work has a written brief for its session: what to build, in what order, and which folders it may touch.

**First wave (three sessions at once)**
- **Part 5: Accounts.** Sign-up, the "Hi, ÔÇª" greeting, `/api/me`, and `admin_user`. New accounts' passwords are stored scrambled (hashed), not as plain text.
- **Part 6: Orders.** Finish saves the order on the server, with prices fixed at the moment of buying. Order numbers start at 1001. Also the My Orders list and the order page.
- **Part 9, first half.** Stock, the stock labels, disabled Add to cart for sold-out products, admin-set prices showing everywhere, and the Admin page.

Each session gets its own folders, so the three won't overwrite each other.

**Second wave**

- **Part 8: Reviews.** It waits for 5, 6 and 9 because it needs names, purchases and the same product pages.
- **Part 9, second half.** Finish takes stock and refuses sold-out items, and the Admin link appears in the menu. This could be a third session or whoever is free first.

A few choices I made that you may notice:
- Saved data goes in a `data/` folder that git ignores.
- The checkout name and postal code are kept on the server between the two checkout steps.
- One review shows as "(1 review)".
- Order dates look like "Oct 5, 2026".
- `admin_user` isn't added to the sign-in page's list of usernames.

## Two questions for you before the sessions start
1. **Should someone be able to fake another person's sign-in?** Right now the shop trusts any username put in the `session-username` cookie, because that's how Swag Labs' tests sign in. With real accounts, that would let one person see another's orders and write reviews as them. I recommend signing the cookie for new accounts only, so Swag Labs' tests still work. The catch: your own tests would need to sign up or sign in through the pages rather than setting that cookie by hand. Part 5 waits on your answer.
2. **May I split Part 9 into two halves?** "Finish refuses sold-out items" changes the same Finish button as Part 6. The admin-only menu link needs the page header that Part 5 is changing. Built all at once, they would collide. I recommend the split; the second half waits on your answer.
